The app ​

The Tiffin app shows everything tiffind is doing, and does everything the command line does. Close its window and it keeps running in the tray (the menu bar on macOS), with your sites, services and shares one click away.

Overview leads with tiffind's health. A green banner means everything is running. An amber one with Set up… means tiffind is fine but this computer is not set up yet (the local CA, .test names or ports 80 and 443). Otherwise the banner lists each part that needs attention, with the reason, and links to Doctor. The status at the foot of the sidebar opens the same list from any screen.

The sidebar groups the screens by what you are doing: Overview and Packages on top, Sites (Projects, PHP, Tools, Share), Run (Services, Databases) and Inspect (Mail, Dumps, Exceptions, Logs). Doctor and Settings stay at the bottom, above the status. The Add button in the header opens Packages from any screen.

Each screen's header says what the screen is for, and Docs beside it opens that screen's page of this guide inside the app. The whole guide is built in, so it works without a network.

Press Ctrl K (⌘ K on macOS), or click the search field at the top, to search screens and actions. Ctrl 1 to Ctrl 9 (⌘ on macOS) jump to the first nine screens, in sidebar order. Ctrl , opens Settings and Ctrl / lists every shortcut. On macOS the menu bar has the same: Go lists every screen, View has the command palette and the sidebar.

Start at sign-in: Settings > Start at login. The app then starts in the tray and starts tiffind with it, so your sites are ready.

The tray menu lists your services (start, stop, logs), your sites (open), what's shared (copy the address, stop sharing), unread mail, Doctor, and restart or quit.

No tray icon on Linux: stock GNOME shows no tray icons. Tiffin then has no icon, and closing its window quits it. The Overview says so. To get the icon, run gnome-extensions enable ubuntu-appindicators@ubuntu.com, then log out and in (install gnome-shell-extension-appindicator first if it is missing).

Quitting the app leaves tiffind serving. Quit and stop tiffind stops both.

Projects ​

Your sites, their PHP, HTTPS and sharing, and the folders you parked. New project… makes a Laravel app or a WordPress site, as tiffin new does, showing each step and the commands' output as they run. Check project… in a site's ⋯ menu compares the project with what Tiffin has (tiffin check).

Tools ​

Small utilities you would otherwise look for on a website. A list on the left has every tool by group; the button at the top left hides it for the full width. Press / to search: type a name, use the arrow keys and press Enter. The tools you star and the ones you opened last sit as buttons beside the search box, one click away, and Ctrl K opens a tool from anywhere.

Most run in the app, on what you type or the file you pick: nothing is sent to tiffind or over the network. The ones that need your system (ports, processes, your environment, files and folders, certificates, web requests the app's web view cannot make, DNS lookups, running code, password hashes) ask tiffind, which does the work as you. Tiffin never uses root for them: it can stop only your own processes, and it writes only files you chose or your own shell startup files. Each also has a tiffin command (see the CLI reference), and the app asks before it stops a process, rewrites files or saves a file.

Custom ​

Your own links, for the pages you open every day (a dashboard, docs, a local admin). Add them in Custom tools, and each shows in this group and opens in your browser. Tiffin keeps them, so they are there after an update.

Every result has a copy button. Files the tools make are saved where you choose in the usual save dialog. Star a tool (next to its name) to keep it in a Favourites group at the top of the list.

Mail ​

Caught mail, shown as it would look, grouped by day; search by sender, recipient or subject. Remote images stay blocked until you allow them.

Dumps ​

Each PHP request to your sites, to send again or copy as curl. From Laravel apps with the package, also its dump() output and queries in order, with N+1, duplicate and slow queries flagged. Narrow the list to one site, or a request to its dumps or its queries.

Exceptions ​

Your sites' errors, grouped: each exception and the line that threw it, how many times and when, with the newest one's stack trace. Open one to copy its trace, read its log, or open its request in Dumps.

Logs ​

Laravel's log as entries, by day, with stack traces a click away.
